# Context Switch Cost

## Definition

The real cost of context switching is not time.

Time is visible, but the deeper cost is the loss of mental continuity.

When the founder switches context, the current mental thread weakens. The founder may still know the visible artifact, but lose the reason, state, decision path, and next meaningful move.

## Mental cost

The founder has to hold several unfinished contexts in mind.

Each open context consumes attention:

- What is this about?
- What changed?
- Who is waiting?
- What did I decide?
- What was I about to do?
- What should not be forgotten?

The mental cost grows when contexts are similar, overlapping, or poorly named.

## Memory cost

The founder must remember relationships that are not stored in any single artifact.

Memory carries the edges between customer, supplier, product, problem, decision, artifact, and conversation.

When those edges fade, artifacts remain but meaning becomes expensive to recover.

## Reporting cost

Reporting requires more than listing activity.

The founder must explain what happened, why it mattered, what changed, what remains unresolved, and how separate fragments connect.

When context is not preserved, reporting becomes a second work session: searching, interpreting, sequencing, and reconstructing.

## Decision cost

Decisions depend on context.

If the founder loses the reasoning around a decision, the decision becomes harder to trust, defend, or revise.

The cost appears later when the founder asks:

- Why did we choose this?
- What was rejected?
- Which constraint mattered?
- Has anything changed enough to revisit it?

## Recovery cost

Recovery is the effort required to become competent inside a context again.

The founder can reopen artifacts quickly and still not be recovered.

Recovery is complete only when the founder understands the active situation well enough to continue without repeating the previous reasoning.

## Compounding cost

Context switching compounds across the day.

One switch can be manageable. Many switches create fatigue because the founder is repeatedly rebuilding mental continuity.

The day becomes expensive not because every interruption is large, but because every interruption leaves a small unpaid recovery debt.

## What the product must respect

Work Memory should treat context switching as a memory problem, not a calendar problem.

The goal is not to count switches. The goal is to reduce the damage each switch does to meaning, reasoning, reporting, decisions, and recovery.
