# Reporting

## Current reporting behaviour

The founder reports work by reconstructing contexts from scattered traces.

The report is assembled from:

- Communication traces.
- Business and operational records.
- Technical artifacts.
- Supplier questions.
- Management messages.
- Meetings.
- Documents.
- Memory.

The founder does not simply report planned tasks. The founder reports what actually happened inside contexts: decisions, progress, blockers, supplier state, product state, reasoning, and changes.

## How reporting happens today

1. A management question or reporting need appears.
2. The founder stops active work.
3. The founder searches recent communication.
4. The founder checks relevant records.
5. The founder recalls technical or product reasoning.
6. The founder reviews notes, documents, and recent memory.
7. The founder assembles a narrative manually.
8. The founder returns to the interrupted context with less continuity than before.

## Why reporting is hard

Reporting is hard because the evidence is real but disconnected.

Communication shows what was said. Records show state. Technical artifacts show design or production evidence. Meetings create decisions. None of these alone explain the context as professional memory.

The founder has to connect the evidence manually.

## What the report needs to contain

- Which contexts moved.
- What changed inside each context.
- Why the work mattered.
- Which supplier, product, customer, problem, artifact, or management question triggered it.
- What was decided.
- What remained blocked.
- What needs to resume next.
- How much effort was invested, when duration matters.

## Current failure modes

- The founder remembers the obligation but not the completed context.
- The founder remembers the artifact but not the reason for opening it.
- The founder remembers the conversation but not the product or decision state behind it.
- The founder remembers the record but not the management explanation.
- The founder estimates work from memory instead of using a reliable context record.

## Ideal reporting behaviour

The founder should report from accumulated context memory, not from reconstruction.

The ideal reporting experience behaves like this:

1. Context is preserved during the day.
2. Captured evidence preserves decisions, blockers, reasoning, and resume points.
3. Context remains connected across people, artifacts, meetings, decisions, and supplier questions.
4. The founder reviews what changed.
5. The founder produces a clear report from actual context.
6. The report does not force the founder to relive the whole day.

## Reporting principle

The report should emerge from context.

It should not depend on the founder searching every container and rebuilding the story from memory.
