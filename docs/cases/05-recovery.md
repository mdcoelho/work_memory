# Recovery

## Scope

This document describes moments where the founder tries to remember context.

The founder recovers context from memory, communication, records, artifacts, documents, meetings, notes, and task traces.

## Remembering what happened yesterday

Today, recovery starts by scanning yesterday's messages, calendar events, documents, records, artifacts, and memory.

The founder reconstructs a sequence of context fragments. The hard part is not finding a single artifact. The hard part is connecting artifacts into the story of what happened.

## Remembering why a decision was made

The founder has to recover the reasoning behind a decision.

The decision might be visible somewhere. The reasoning is often distributed across conversations, constraints, product state, supplier information, and meeting outcomes.

Recovery fails when the decision exists but the context behind it was never preserved.

## Remembering which context this belonged to

The founder works across multiple contexts that coexist inside the same day and sometimes inside the same container.

The same customer, product, supplier, meeting, record, model, or document can belong to different contexts for different reasons.

Recovery requires knowing which context the artifact served.

## Remembering how much effort was invested

When effort matters, the founder reconstructs it from the sequence of work: messages answered, records checked, artifacts reviewed, supplier follow-up, management questions, and meetings.

The problem is not only duration. The founder needs to explain what the effort meant.

## Remembering where work stopped

The founder recovers the stop point by reopening relevant evidence and rebuilding the prior mental state.

Common stop-point failures:

- The last message exists but not the answer forming in the founder's head.
- The record exists but not why it was being checked.
- The artifact exists but not the supplier, customer, product, or decision context that triggered it.
- The meeting happened but its effect on other contexts is unclear.

## Remembering what to report

The founder reports completed work, progress, blockers, decisions, and reasoning.

Recovery involves pulling together progress, decisions, blockers, supplier context, product context, artifact work, and management questions.

## How recovery happens today

1. Remember that something happened.
2. Search communication for the trail.
3. Check records for state.
4. Reopen artifacts for technical or product state.
5. Review notes, documents, and calendar.
6. Recreate the reasoning between containers.
7. Estimate what changed and where work stopped.

## Recovery failures

- The founder finds the artifact but not the context.
- The founder remembers the decision but not the reasoning.
- The founder remembers the person but not the problem.
- The founder remembers the product but not the active constraint.
- The founder remembers the meeting but not what it changed.

## Recovery principle

Recovery should start from context, not from searching every container.
